Before you send

What to include so the first reply is useful

We answer every enquiry with a plan rather than a brochure, and the usefulness of that plan depends almost entirely on what you send. None of this is mandatory — send what you have and we will ask about the rest.

If you are a student

Your branch, your college's submission date, and anything your guide has already fixed. If you have a synopsis, an abstract or an IEEE paper, attach it. If you have no project idea yet, say so plainly — that is a completely normal starting point, and we will come back with options that suit your branch and your deadline rather than whatever is quickest to build.

The final year project help form asks for all of this directly, so it gets you a more exact answer than a plain message will.

If you are hiring us for commercial work

What the software has to do, who will use it, when you need it live, and a budget range if you have one. A rough range is not a weakness to give away — it tells us whether to propose the full build or a smaller first version, which saves a whole round of back and forth. If the details are sensitive, send the NDA first and we will sign it before you share anything.

How engagements are structured is set out on the hire a freelance full stack developer page.

If you are not sure what you need yet

Plenty of first messages are a paragraph describing a problem, not a specification. That is fine, and often more useful. Describe the problem and the constraint you are stuck on, and we will tell you what we would build, roughly what it would cost, and whether we are the right people for it at all — including when the answer is no.

If you would rather see the range first, browse all development services.
